# Ramp Tour at Cranky Dorkfest 2026

It's not too often that the big three airlines in the U.S., or the "US3," can agree on much of anything. Yet this past weekend they did just that, joining Southwest at LAX by offering plane enthusiasts a rare opportunity to tour their aircraft just for the fun of it. Whether it was talking shop while sitting left seat in the flight deck, exploring the crew rest facilities on a Dreamliner, or seeing what type of seat actually fits your current physique, there was so much on offer during the LAX ramp tour at this year's Cranky Dorkfest 2026.

Dorkfest takes place the second or third weekend in September each year at LAX. In coordination with Los Angeles World Airports, the event includes (for those lucky enough to be selected ahead of time) a morning ramp tour with aircraft on static display. This year we had 5: United showcasing a B789 Dreamliner; American put forth an A321XLR and the AirCal heritage B738; Delta joined in with their special 100th Anniversary A321neo; and Southwest rounded out the pack with their patriotic Independence One 737 Max. All together this represented the four largest carriers in the United States making the event truly one-of-a-kind.

It's essential to sign up early as soon as the registration email comes out from Cranky Flier. Here they ask for your Driver's License information so that a background check may be conducted. Once clear and confirmed to attend, I received an email about parking and other various particulars and before I knew it, it was time to gear up and go!

The ramp tour took place this year on Saturday, September 19th, from 8am to noon. The weather was perfect for a day outdoors: mid-70 degrees with light westerly winds and a shallow marine layer that burned off by lunchtime. The airport provided a DJ playing music, Randy's Doughnuts, coffee, and plenty of water for all, including special LAX canteens. Attendees lined up at the airstairs to tour each of the five aircraft on display, and a shuttle was even on site giving people a chance to drive around LAX Airport beyond the confines of the Flight Path Museum where the event took place.

Unfortunately I didn't have time to get on one of the field tours as I was more interested in getting inside the planes themselves. Next year I plan on arriving as soon as it opens, maybe even earlier around 7:30am, in order to take advantage of everything on offer. The lines moved slowly since the crews showing off their aircraft only allowed about 5 to ten people onboard at a given time. Captains, first officers, and flight attendants were all excited to be there too, proudly showing off their fleet's finest. It was fun to be able to converse with them about why they love flying so much.

The ramp tour had people of all ages, young and old alike. Many attendees came equipped with expensive cameras to capture all the action. Goodies were handed out at the base of the airstairs and included things like posters, trading cards, knicknacs etc. Between the enthusiasm of the various crews, the plethora of complimentary items (which also included mobile restrooms that were kept spotless), and the willingness of the airlines themselves to donate their aircraft to this event, all in all it felt like something out of Avgeek's dream!

The only aircraft that stood out to me was the American 321XLR. That was the only one boarding from the rear L2 door, so we entered from the back. Economy looked good with bright IFE screens, so props to AA for reinstating seatback entertainment to their fleet! Premium Economy felt to me like the best value, with open recliners and plenty of legroom especially at the bulkhead in row 11. However I was surprised by Flagship Business Class at the front of the plane and just how claustrophobic it felt. Herringbone configurations are an unfortunate reality of many narrowbody business class cabins these days, but this one really felt tight especially around the shoulders and head. Lastly, and not to take away from the excitement of a new aircraft joining the fleet, but the overall quality of the AA 321XLR cabin left something to be desired. Many seats were already showing their age despite the plane having been in service since only December last year.

All in all, the ramp tour was the highlight of the Cranky Dorkfest and SpotLAX weekend. It felt almost like being at a private airshow with a bunch of fellow friends. My hat is off to Brett Snyder at Cranky Flier and everyone at LAWA for orchestrating and putting together such a rad event! Kudos and see you back there in 2027!!
